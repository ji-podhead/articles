The two-phase entropy gate - the router is already in the response

Figure 1 · The model's own token distribution decides: one threshold, no classifier, no labeled data, no second inference system.
Request
any proxied call, e.g. "what's the capital of France?" next to "refactor this payment module"
→phase 1: a full normal call -
bandit routing, quota tracking, feedback
Phase 1 - small routing model
cheap model with logprobs: true, top_logprobs: 5 - the top-5 token candidates with probabilities at every position, the complete uncertainty signal, free.
→
Normalized entropy, per response
1. top-k logprobs back to probabilities p_i · 2. Shannon entropy H = -Σ p_i · log₂(p_i) · 3. normalize H_norm = H / log₂(k) - 0 means the model was certain, 1 means a uniform coin flip · 4. average over all output tokens.
Average below 0.45 (ours, configurable)
The cheap model was confident - serve its response directly. Easy requests get faster (small model) at cheap-model price.
At or above the threshold
Discard the entire generated answer and re-route the original request through the normal chain to the reasoning model. Escalation pays twice - the price of not having a trained router.
Fail-open by design
If phase 1 errors out (400 included), the request falls through to normal routing. A chain whose providers all reject the parameter degrades to "no cascade", never to "no service" - the gate must never be a single point of failure.
Streaming variant: pull the ripcord mid-flight
A sliding window (10 tokens, valid after the first 5) tracks the running average; the moment it crosses the threshold, the stream is cancelled and re-routed - the speculative-decoding trick applied to cost: draft optimistically, verify cheaply.
The honest arithmetic
Every request pays phase-1 tokens; hard requests get slower because you only know they're hard after the cheap model tried. If 90% of traffic is easy (typical for mixed agent workloads), you pay ~10% of cheap-model price for 90% of requests instead of 100% of big-model price. The threshold is the risk dial: lower it for more quality at more cost, raise it to save more - and quietly keep more marginal answers.
