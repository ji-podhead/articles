From rule bases to ReAct: 40 years of knowledge-based AI

The hardware changed beyond recognition - the two-component playbook never left: a knowledge base, and an inference machine that processes it.
1965 Zadeh
Fuzzy sets: degrees of truth, membership grades between 0 and 1 instead of crisp boundaries - reasoning under imprecision.
1965-1976 DENDRAL, MYCIN
Expert systems encode the specialist: DENDRAL infers molecular structure, MYCIN recommends antibiotics.
1980s XCON at DEC
Rule-based configurer saving DEC millions a year, running on Lisp machines the size of a small fridge. Then the stall: the knowledge acquisition bottleneck - manual, brittle, expensive - and systems that could not gracefully say "I do not know".
2009 Wooldridge
Agents: no universal definition, but autonomy is the must - a system situated in an environment, acting independently toward goals. Statefulness (stored past shaping future decisions) becomes the precondition for memory.
2020 Lewis et al.
RAG: the curated collection is the knowledge base again - and ingestion pipelines become the first credible attack on the acquisition bottleneck.
2022 → Yao et al.
ReAct: the reasoning-acting loop with an LLM in the interpreter seat; proactive agents add anticipation. Agentic RAG re-imports what worked: explicit knowledge, explicit rules, explicit verification.
The constant: knowledge base + inference machine
rule base → curated vector collection · rule interpreter → retrieval verifier chaining evidence · fuzzy degrees of truth → relevance scores and membership-style gates · "confidently wrong" outside its competence → hallucination, the grounding problem under a new name
Hallucination is the return of the 1980s grounding problem - and verification, not vibes, is still the fix.
